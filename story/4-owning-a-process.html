Chapter 4·Jan 2024 – May 2025Snowflake

Owning a process, start to finish

In short: as Partner Process Specialist I owned the monthly process for partners' cloud funds, $1.5M to $3M a quarter, and replaced its daily Excel matching with Python.

At Snowflake, a whole process was mine. Partners received cloud funds, and I owned the monthly cycle that managed them: $1.5 million to $3 million a quarter, with the most requests landing at the start of each month.

Reports that wouldn't join

Every cycle looked like a small data pipeline. I pulled data with SQL from Snowhouse and from Workday, downloaded reports from sources that couldn't be linked to each other, matched them up, calculated the figures and entered the results in the partner information system.

The matching was done by hand in Excel. It needed updating every day, and it took the whole month.

Writing the rules down in code

I moved the matching into Python, in Snowflake notebooks, with the formulas and matching rules defined in the code. Instead of updating a spreadsheet every day, I ran the script once, at the end of the week.

The job wasn't only data. When a partner's funds were stuck, for example because the partner hadn't sent the documents needed to submit them, I ran the meetings to get them moving.

The last day of the fiscal year

At fiscal-year end there was a backlog of about 200 to 300 pending fund requests. I stayed on for a full day, while travelling, and entered every one of them, so the backlog was clear and the budget was less affected.

I had the freedom to choose my own tools and start my own projects, as long as the work got done. It's still one of the best jobs I've had.
