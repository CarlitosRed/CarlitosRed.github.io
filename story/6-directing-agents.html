Chapter 6·2026 – nowToday

Learning to work with AI agents

A paid document-AI prototype, a public agent framework and Job Search HQ. On the two public projects I set goals, make decisions and test the results; AI agents write the code.

A paid prototype

In September 2026 I took on a paid freelance project. A client had a system that reads incoming orders with document AI, and I delivered the investigation, the business logic and a prototype. The client and the terms stay private.

Who checks the agents?

In October 2026 I started a different kind of project. AI coding agents from two companies, Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's Codex, would write the code. My part was the one I'd liked best at Snowflake: looking after the process. I set the goals and the limits, made recorded decisions and approved changes.

The rule that mattered most: the one who did the work shouldn't be the one who checks it. So one company's agent wrote a change and the other company's agent reviewed it. The framework records review at an exact version and checks the evidence required by the project's configuration. Review and owner approval requirements depend on the tier and vendor setup.

Offline tests and CI help check changes before they are used.

The second reviewer caught bugs the author's own tests had missed, including one that could silently corrupt data.

Writing failures down

I'm learning this as I go, so mistakes are part of the work. Process failures go into a log with its root cause and fix, and every rule names what enforces it: the gate, the tests, a template, or just advice. As of 21 October 2026 that was 29 rules and 29 logged failures.

One entry shows why this matters. Our notes said one agent's shell commands failed every single time. The agents re-tested it, the failure was gone, and the note was corrected with the date and the observed status.

The framework is public, with pull requests and tagged releases you can inspect. You can read it on GitHub.

A tool I use myself

Job Search HQ puts my job tracker, evidence-guided document drafts and downloads in one place. It runs on my PC and opens on my phone. I set the goals, tested it on my phone and approved changes while Claude and Codex agents wrote the code.

The public code and setup include fictional examples and offline tests. Drafts still need a person to check every claim; the tool cannot prove one true. I submit applications myself.

What connects the chapters

Looking back, it's been the same job each time, at a bigger scale. Find where knowledge gets lost, write it down, make the process repeatable, and check the result before anyone depends on it. First that was a new hire on a call, then a support team, then a monthly funds process. Now it's AI agents.

I'm also studying Data Science Engineering at LEAD University. The next chapter, I hope, is on a team that builds with AI agents, where I can keep learning and help make sure the work is right.
