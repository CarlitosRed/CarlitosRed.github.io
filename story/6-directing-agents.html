Chapter 6·2026 – nowToday

Learning to work with AI agents

In short: a paid prototype for a client's document-AI system, then a public project where AI agents write the code, and I set the rules and give the final yes.

A paid prototype

In September 2026 I took on a paid freelance project. A client had a system that reads incoming orders with document AI, and I delivered the investigation, the business logic and a prototype. The client and the terms stay private.

Who checks the agents?

In October 2026 I started a different kind of project. AI coding agents from two companies, Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's Codex, would write the code. My part was the one I'd liked best at Snowflake: looking after the process. I set the goals and the limits, made the calls and gave the final approval on every change.

The rule that mattered most: the one who did the work shouldn't be the one who checks it. So one company's agent wrote a change and the other company's agent reviewed it. A merge gate refuses any change that's missing that review at the exact version, the evidence it requires in the pull request, my approval, or a clean secrets scan.

About 500 offline tests have to pass before anything lands (529 as of 26 October 2026).

The second reviewer caught bugs the author's own tests had missed, including one that could silently corrupt data.

Writing failures down

I'm learning this as I go, so mistakes are part of the work. Every process failure goes into a log with its root cause and fix, and every rule names what enforces it: the gate, the tests, a template, or just advice. As of 21 October 2026 that was 29 rules and 29 logged failures.

One entry shows why this matters. Our notes said one agent's shell commands failed every single time. The agents re-tested it, the failure was gone, and the note was corrected with the date and the cause.

The project is public: 97 pull requests merged and 11 tagged releases as of 26 October 2026. You can read it on GitHub.

What connects the chapters

Looking back, it's been the same job each time, at a bigger scale. Find where knowledge gets lost, write it down, make the process repeatable, and check the result before anyone depends on it. First that was a new hire on a call, then a support team, then a monthly funds process. Now it's AI agents.

I'm also studying Data Science Engineering at LEAD University. The next chapter, I hope, is on a team that builds with AI agents, where I can keep learning and help make sure the work is right.
