Chapter 2·2021 – 2023Amadeus

The product nobody had written down

In short: my team inherited software with no documentation. I built the knowledge base we worked from, trained new hires, and then moved into a Data Analyst role.

At Amadeus I started in technical support for hospitality software built on Salesforce, used by large hotel chains. Calls, emails and chats, again, but now the problems were inside the product: user access, document templates mapped to Salesforce fields, and calls of up to an hour with clients to review how they had set up their reports.

Knowledge that didn't come with the product

Amadeus had bought the product, and the know-how didn't come with it. There was no documentation, and we still had to support it.

So I built a knowledge base on OneNote and SharePoint, with an index, organised like a documentation website. To keep it current, I ran weekly sessions with my colleagues to hear about new issues, and then I wrote them up. Over time, documenting a new issue became team policy: whoever found it wrote it down.

I also proposed splitting support into subject-matter groups, so each person handled the kind of issue they were best at. My manager approved it, and it stayed in place after I left. Along the way I trained about four new hires, in two waves.

It was the part of the job I enjoyed most: finding out what people had discovered, and making sure the next person didn't have to discover it again.

From answering cases to reading them

In my second year I moved into a Data Analyst role. Instead of solving cases, I analysed them: what the issues were, how one person had fixed a case, and why that was faster than someone else's way. I wrote Python scripts for the analysis and used R for the charts, and the findings went back into the knowledge base.

One finding was that some people were simply better at certain kinds of cases. Rather than share cases out equally, I suggested giving each person more of the case types they were good at.

Choosing the next door

Being on the phone and tied to a desk all day was the hardest part for me. Around that time, a manager from Amadeus's IT division saw my work and offered me a role there. I turned it down for an offer from VMware, which gave me something I wanted: the flexibility to work while travelling.

Tools in this chapterSalesforce (user access, roles, formulas, flows, document templates), OneNote, SharePoint, Python, R.
