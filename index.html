Case 01 Program Owner Oct 2026 Checkable in public

A public governance framework for teams of AI coding agents

When two AI vendors' agents write and review each other's code, someone has to decide what "done" means and make it hard to skip. I directed Claude and Codex agents to build that system: roles, a merge gate, a review loop, a rules register and a log of process failures.

  • A merge gate that refuses to merge without a tier label, required evidence in the pull request, a review from the other vendor at the exact commit, the owner's approval, and a secrets scan.
  • Cross-vendor review (Claude writes, Codex reviews) caught real defects the author's own tests missed, including a silent data-corruption bug and a gap in CI trust.
  • About 500 offline tests across 10 suites must pass in CI before any change can merge (as of Oct 2026).
  • 29 rules, each naming what enforces it, and a 29-row failure log with root cause and fix (as of 2026-10-21); 97 pull requests merged and 11 tagged releases (as of 2026-10-26).

My part (self-reported): I set the goals, made the recorded decisions and gave the final approval on each change. The agents wrote the code.

Case 03 Snowflake Jan 2024 – May 2025 Self-reported

Automating a partner-funds process worth $1.5M–$3M a quarter

I owned the monthly process for managing partners' cloud funds. The data lived in places that could not be joined at the source, and the matching was done by hand in Excel, updated every day through the month.

  • Pulled data with SQL from Snowhouse and from Workday, matched and linked the reports, calculated the figures and entered them in the partner information system.
  • Replaced the manual Excel matching with Python scripts in Snowflake notebooks, run once at the end of the week.
  • At fiscal-year end, entered the full backlog of about 200 to 300 pending fund requests in one day, so the budget was less affected.

06 / How I work

Rules I hold the agents to, and myself.

Evidence first

A claim needs a source. This site follows the same rule: every line traces to a written record, and the parts you can check yourself are marked.

Re-test what's written down

A tool failure was documented as permanent. It was re-tested, no longer happened, and the record was corrected with the cause logged.

The author never reviews their own work

One AI vendor writes, the other reviews, and nothing merges without that review at the exact commit.

Decisions in writing

Decisions are dated and recorded; failures go in a log with root cause and fix. A decision that isn't written down doesn't exist.
